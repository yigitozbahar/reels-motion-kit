# Format guide: interactive horror Reels

The rules a part should follow, distilled from studying successful interactive horror reels (several accounts, a
finished 5-part series among them). They describe structure and timing only: every story here is original.

## The beat map of a ~70 s part

| s | beat | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | hook | one sentence with a single impossible detail, said to "you" |
| 5–10 | rule | an alert, a message or a note sets the rule of the game |
| 10–29 | escalation | a new fact every 3–5 s; the picture changes on almost every sentence |
| 29–53 | two sources | two sources that contradict each other: who do you trust? |
| 53–58 | squeeze | the threat closes in: there are seconds left |
| 58–67 | A/B | both options read aloud, then the question |
| 67–70 | call | comment A or B, follow for the next part |

Narration runs at about 2 words a second in English. Turkish with Emel and `--gap=0.5` runs at about 1.9 words a
second: 110–140 words fill a 60–85 s part.

## Series mechanics

- Parts 2+ open on the winning option: on screen "you chose A" plus the option's text, spoken "most of you chose A".
  Never percentages or vote counts.
- Each part **reveals one secret and adds one new threat**.
- Write the options so both feel defensible. Lopsided options kill the comments.
- The final part says it's the final part and which option won. It explains the opening mystery, and its last image
  calls back to part 1's first image (the same place, now understood). Then an end card: the title, END, "you chose
  the ending", "follow for the next story".
- Keep the caption style the same across every part of a series.

## Rules

1. The first sentence is the hook: ≤5 s, second person, one impossible detail.
2. Set the rule in the first 20–30 s (a phone, a note, an announcement).
3. Short sentences; something new, and a new shot, every 3–5 s.
4. Two contradicting sources build the dilemma; both options plausible and risky; check each line's speaker logic
   ("who wrote this, and why would they say it?").
5. Close in the last 12–18 s: read the options → the card → "Yorumlara A ya da B yaz" → "takipte kal". The card stays
   up until the end.
6. Parts 2+ open with "Çoğunuz A'yı seçti" plus the option's text.
7. The final part pays off part 1's hook and ends on a "SON" card.
8. 60–85 s per part.
9. Captions: Anton, upper case, centred, the current word in red. Information arrives on screen through objects
   (phones, notes, documents); dialogue in bubbles or chat messages.
10. First-person POV: the viewer's hands, their phone, their door. A dark palette with one accent colour.
11. A persistent HUD (a clock, a counter) is optional: a style decision per concept.
